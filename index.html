FIRST VISIT KITA working concept by Keshvi Pipwala for Storytime's Software Engineer, Product & Growth role

Get every brand to its first creator visit faster.

Your posting lists the problems this role owns. I picked five of them and made a working version of each, on made-up New York data, so we can argue about real tradeoffs instead of slides. Every screen below reacts to what you change.

If you have one minute

The whole argument, then the tools
The problem

Brands pay month to month with no long-term contract. A brand that gets no creator visit in its first week can decide influencer marketing doesn't work for it and leave before the product proves itself.

What I'd ship first

A setup screen that forecasts days to first visit and fixes weak settings in one tap, plus a 72-hour rescue for campaigns with no accepts.

How we'd know

Median hours from launch to first redeemed visit, by business type and neighborhood. One number the team can move and check weekly.

01 · Brand activation

A campaign setup that shows the brand what happens next

A new brand picks reach tiers and an offer value. Before they launch, the setup tells them how many creators nearby will see the offer, when the first visit will probably come, and which setting is holding them back.

Start from one sentence

AI intake with a confidence gate

Most owners won't think in reach tiers. Let them describe the goal in their own words, fill the setup from it, and ask them to confirm only what the parser isn't sure about.

    How this estimate works
    • A creator sees the offer if they're in a chosen tier, inside the radius, active in the last 45 days, and post about this kind of business.
    • Chance of accepting = tier base rate (Nano 35%, Core 28%, Mid 16%) × offer vs. a tier anchor ($10 / $20 / $35) × distance decay (halves about every 1.4 km) × audience fit.
    • Expected posts use each creator's past post rate. New creators are assumed to post 70% of the time.
    • Days to first visit treat visits as random arrivals at the forecast monthly rate, plus 2 days for the creator to book.
    • All rates are my placeholders. With real acceptance logs this becomes a fitted model per tier and neighborhood.

    03 · Content approval

    Approve the posts it's sure about. Send the rest to a person.

    After a visit, someone has to confirm the creator posted what was promised. This checker scores each post and auto-approves only above a confidence threshold. It's tested against 72 posts a person already labeled, so the precision figure below is measured, not claimed. Missing ad disclosure always goes to review, since FTC rules require paid posts to say they're paid.

    Precision on auto-approved postsShare auto-approved
    What the checker looks at
    • Brand account tagged, location tagged, caption names the business, paid-post disclosure present, posted within 7 days of the visit, and no other venue tagged.
    • The score is a weighted sum passed through a logistic curve. Disclosure and timing carry the most weight because they're the hardest to fix after the fact.
    • Labels include a few judgment calls a rule can't see, like a photo taken somewhere else. Those are why precision drops as the threshold goes down.
    • In production I'd add an LLM pass on the caption and image for the long tail, with the rule score as the fallback if the model call fails.

      What I know, what I assumed, and what I'd ask for

      Known, from public sources

      • SourceBrands launch by choosing reach tiers and an offer value, and the platform handles matching. Storytime: how it works
      • SourceThe founders describe 5k-15k-follower creators as the sweet spot, and weight location over follower count. Brands pay a flat monthly subscription with no long-term contract. QSR Magazine, July 2025
      • SourceThe role covers activation experiments, creator matching, content approval, brand reporting and internal tools. Job posting

      Assumed, and how I'd check it

      • AssumedAcceptance rates and offer anchors per tier. Check against 90 days of offer and acceptance logs.
      • AssumedThe funnel shape and the step names. Check against real signup events, then fix the spec to match what's already tracked.
      • AssumedWhat counts as a complete post. Confirm with the community team, who approve posts today.

      My first 90 days, against your "what success looks like" list

      • Weeks 1-3Sit with partnerships, community and support. Watch five brands set up a campaign. Replace my assumed funnel with the real events and fix the gaps in tracking.
      • Weeks 4-8Ship the first production change on the biggest real drop in brand setup, measured against the step before it. Then iterate on it once with the data.
      • Weeks 9-12Build one internal tool the team asks for twice, likely the stalled-brand queue or post review, and track the hours it saves.

      Decisions I made, and why

      • ChoiceDistance outweighs reach in matching. A creator two blocks away can come back and bring friends. One across the river usually visits once.
      • ChoicePost check defaults to a 0.85 threshold. A wrongly approved post costs a brand's trust, and a review costs a minute of someone's time.
      • ChoiceThe setup screen forecasts days to first visit, because a brand that sees no visits in week one churns before it sees any value.
